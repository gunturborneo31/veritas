# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The core moment is vivid.** A recipient taps a WhatsApp link, gets past a PIN or a time lock, blows out candles into the mic, scratches a card, and hits "yes" or a reply button. You can describe that moment and build backwards from it, which is the right way round.
- **There is very little friction for either side.** The buyer needs no login, sees a watermarked preview before paying and pays by QRIS. The recipient needs no account. Link and QR are how Indonesians already pass things around.
- **The pricing is easy to understand.** One payment buys one page with no subscription. At Rp25.000–Rp35.000 it is an impulse purchase.
- **It already exists.** The product is built, so it can be tested with real buyers.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It has no focus.** Greetings and event invitations are two different products. One is an emotional impulse gift for one person. The other is planned logistics for hundreds of guests, with RSVP, guest lists and envelopes. On top of that come ~20 categories, 40+ templates and 20+ components, with group mode, Paket 2 and AI characters on the roadmap. A solo developer spread this thin cannot get any single path to brilliant. I cannot say "the one thing" in one sentence, and the founder needs to be able to.
2. **Manual payment creates a seam at the emotional peak.** The buyer has just finished something personal, often on a deadline (a birthday tonight, a "nembak" moment). If the static-QRIS fallback leaves them waiting for an admin to confirm, the magic dies, and so does trust. Content locked after payment adds a second risk: one typo in a partner's name, with no way to edit, can turn a gift into a regret and a refund request.
3. **The craft in the recipient's first five seconds is unproven.** The recipient never chose Kitoo. They judge it by load speed on a cheap Android phone over mobile data, by whether the mic permission prompt feels creepy, and by whether a PIN gate confuses them before they see anything. With this many interactive components there are many places for small details to go wrong. One janky open spoils the moment and costs the referral.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is Kitoo? Is it greetings or invitations?
- Walk me through the first five minutes, from landing on the site to holding a paid link. How long does it take on a phone, and where do people stop?
- If you had to cut to 5 templates and 6 components, which would stay? What does the open-rate and reply data say?
- What happens, minute by minute, when dynamic QRIS fails at 23:00?
- Why freeze content with no edits at all? Would a short edit window cost you anything real?
- What does a recipient see on a low-end phone with a weak signal?
- How do buyers find you? This is not answered yet. The size of the market and what it costs to win a customer are open questions for /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer. The prices Lovio charges are an open question for /founder-competitors. Whether a Rp25.000 page covers its costs is an open question for /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one.** Launch greetings only (or invitations only) with the 5–8 strongest templates. Hide the rest until the data earns them a place.
2. **Make payment instant.** Get dynamic QRIS reliable before scaling, or state a guaranteed confirmation time for the fallback. Add a short edit window after payment, or a mandatory final-check screen before it.
3. **Make the recipient open flawless.** Set a load-time target on a low-end Android over 3G/4G, write clear permission copy, and test the open flow with real recipients.
4. **Measure the full loop**, from drafts to paid to recipient opened to recipient replied, on a small real launch before building group mode, Paket 2 or AI features.
