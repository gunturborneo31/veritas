# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Effort and time are already low.** No login, ready-made templates, a mobile editor, and QRIS payment take away most of the friction for a buyer who can't design. The recipient needs no account either. These are the two parts of the value equation where most offers fail, and Kitoo handles both well.
- **The watermarked preview builds belief.** The buyer sees exactly what they will get before paying. That is the best proof a product with no track record can offer, and it raises the buyer's sense that this will work.
- **The dream outcome is emotional and easy to picture.** The buyer pictures the moment the recipient opens it, blows out the candles or answers "yes". The interactive parts (PIN gate, time lock, quiz, scratch card, voice message) are cheap to deliver again and again and feel valuable, which is the right material for a stack.
- **The invitation side has a real, recurring pain.** Weddings, aqiqah and khitanan are events where families already spend money. RSVP, a digital envelope and a personal link per guest solve real logistics problems.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A weak market on the greetings side.** The pain is "nice to have", not "massive", and a Rp25.000 sale needs very high volume. How customers will find it is still undecided. If each buyer costs more to win than one sale earns, the business loses money on every greeting. Payback depends on CAC and repeat purchases, and both are unknown (/founder-cfo, /founder-consumer).
2. **It slides into competing on price.** Kitoo's stated differentiators (more categories, a future group mode, a future Paket 2) are mostly not built yet, and "more categories" alone does not make buyers feel stupid saying no. If Lovio.id and the invitation sellers offer similar features, Kitoo is a commodity sold at Rp25.000 (/founder-competitors).
3. **The terms add sacrifice without a reason.** Freezing content after payment means one typo in a wedding invitation is a disaster for the buyer. There is no guarantee, and one payment covers only one recipient. Each of these lowers the perceived value right at the moment of paying.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which segment comes first, greetings or invitations? They have different buyers, different prices and different channels.
- How do the first 100 paying buyers arrive, and what will each one cost?
- What is the guarantee? For example: "Your recipient opens it, or we refund you", or free edits until the first open.
- What price does Lovio.id charge, and what do the invitation sellers charge? (/founder-competitors)
- Could the wedding package cost Rp150.000+ if it included guest-list import, unlimited edits until the event and a WhatsApp send helper?
- What share of buyers buy a second time (the next birthday, Lebaran)?
- What do you already know about how many drafts reach preview, and how many previews end in a payment?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one market and say why.** I would start with event invitations: the pain is bigger, the budget is bigger, and the occasions are dated. Show that real buyers in it will pay (/founder-consumer).
2. **Rebuild the offer as a stack, not a single page.** Allow edits until the first open or until the event date, add a clear guarantee the CFO can afford, add honest bonuses (for example a guest-list sender or a certificate), and give it a named package with real urgency around Lebaran and wedding season (/founder-offer).
3. **Unit economics that hold up.** Bring a CAC estimate and an acquisition channel that pays for itself within the first purchase, or within a repeat purchase that has evidence behind it (/founder-cfo).
4. **A competitor price map.** It must show Kitoo is not the cheap option, but the obvious one at its price (/founder-competitors).
